Lately, for some reason, I keep seeing clips of Aldi Taher all over my timeline.

And honestly… the more I watch him, the more I feel like this guy is actually really interesting.

Back then, people mostly saw him as weird, random, overly absurd, or even annoying.

But now?

Somehow, a lot of people are starting to genuinely like him.

And I think there is a pretty interesting reason behind that.

### All Burgers Belong to Allah

One of his most iconic quotes is:

> All burgers belong to Allah.

Which is objectively hilarious because… he was literally promoting burgers at the time wkwkwk.

What makes it even funnier is that he also casually promotes other burger brands too:
- Burger Bangor,
- Burger King,
- even Yupi Burger 😭

If you think about it from a marketing perspective, it makes absolutely no sense. Most people try to subtly outperform competitors, or at least focus entirely on selling their own products.

But Aldi Taher’s vibe is more like:
> well… all blessings comes from Allah anyway.

And weirdly enough, that mindset feels incredibly peaceful.

### “Real Loss Only Exists in Hell”

There was also a moment when someone asked him:
> What if your business loses money?

And he answered:
> That’s not a real loss. Real loss only exists in hell. If your business fails, maybe that’s just the amount of blessings Allah gave you.

At first, it sounds funny.

But the more I think about it, the more I realize how expensive that mindset actually is.

A lot of us tie our self-worth too closely to outcomes:
- fail once → suddenly feel useless,
- business slows down → feel like life is ruined,
- get rejected → feel like the world is over.

Even though maybe… not everything is supposed to go exactly the way we planned.

Sometimes we already gave our best effort, and the results still do not match our expectations. And maybe, for that moment, that was simply our portion of blessings.

That does not mean life is over.

### There Is Something Calming About People Who Are Not Defensive

One thing I genuinely like about him is how he responds to criticism.

At one point, an ustadz corrected him for saying things like:
> stress belongs to Allah

because it was considered inappropriate in terms of adab.

And his response was surprisingly calm:
> MasyaAllah Barakallah, thank you ustadz for reminding me…

No anger.  
No defensiveness.  
No trying to prove himself right.

Meanwhile, the internet today is filled with people who:
- cannot handle criticism,
- are overly protective of their ego,
- or instantly attack back when corrected.

So seeing someone respond that calmly feels… refreshing.

### Maybe We Take Life Too Seriously

And the more I think about it, the more I realize something.

Maybe the reason people are starting to like Aldi Taher is because he looks like someone who has already made peace with himself.

He does not seem obsessed with maintaining an image of being:
- cool,
- intellectual,
- or perfectly normal all the time.

He just lives his life, jokes around, works, accepts criticism, and keeps moving like usual.

And somehow, in the middle of an internet culture built around validation and image management, people like that feel rare.

His whole vibe is basically:
> it is what it is wkwkwk.

And maybe that is exactly why people find him comforting.

### We Live in an Era Where Everyone Tries Too Hard to Be “Enough”

Sometimes I feel like people do not love Aldi Taher just because he is funny.

People like him because he looks free.

Free from the pressure to always look cool.  
Free from the pressure to always sound smart.  
Free from the fear of constantly being judged online.

Meanwhile, many of us live the exact opposite way.

We spend so much energy building personas:
- trying to look productive,
- trying to look successful,
- trying to have the perfect opinions,
- trying to be relatable but not weird,
- trying to be confident without looking arrogant.

It is exhausting.

And maybe that is why seeing someone who can laugh at himself, accept criticism peacefully, continue living normally despite constantly becoming memes, and simply move on with life… feels strangely comforting.

As if he is reminding us:
> life does not have to be that serious.

Maybe that is why Aldi Taher feels calming to so many people.

Because while many of us are still trying so hard to appear perfect on the internet, he looks like someone who got tired of chasing validation a long time ago.

And maybe… peace really is that simple.
